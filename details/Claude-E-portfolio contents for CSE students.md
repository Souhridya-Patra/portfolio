Here's a breakdown of what each section should actually contain at your stage:

**Hero / About me** is your first impression. Keep the tagline honest — something like "2nd year CSE student passionate about [web dev / systems / AI]." Add your GitHub and LinkedIn links prominently.

**Projects are the most critical section.** Recruiters and seniors look here first. Even if your projects are small, document them properly — explain the problem you solved, not just the tech stack. A GitHub link is a must. 2–4 well-documented projects beat a long list of vague ones.

**Skills** should be honest. At year 2, list only what you've actually used. Avoid padding with tools you only installed. Split into Programming Languages, Tools/Platforms, and CS Concepts.

**Education** is straightforward — your CGPA, college name, and a line about relevant coursework (Data Structures, Computer Networks, etc.) adds context for viewers who may not know your curriculum.

**Experience / Activities** — if you don't have an internship yet, that's completely fine. College clubs, hackathon participation, open-source contributions, and technical events all belong here. They signal initiative.

**Certifications** from platforms like NPTEL, Coursera, or even YouTube-based structured learning can go here, along with your competitive coding profiles (LeetCode, Codeforces).

**Blog / Writing** is optional but a strong differentiator if you enjoy explaining things — even one or two technical posts show communication skills.

**Resume + Contact** — always have a downloadable, up-to-date PDF resume and a working contact method.

One important note: as a 2nd year student, your portfolio doesn't need to look exhaustive. Authenticity and clarity matter more than quantity. Recruiters who visit student portfolios expect work-in-progress — what they look for is genuine effort and clear communication.
